How I made Robie Hoodie: the prompts and the edit

A 28-second reel: Robie Hoodie and his Merry Men rob the richest gated community in town, shoot the money into the doors of the hood, and get arrested like rap stars. Here are the 5 steps and the prompts I pasted.

No music here. The song is added on Instagram. Everything you see is AI-generated.

This is CASE 002 of my series Once Upon a Crime: fairy-tale characters as real-world criminals. The old tale's ending becomes the punchline. Here: took from the rich, gave to the poor, kept the grills.

What you need

  • An image model that does real skin. I used GPT Image 2.5 inside Dreamina: 30 points per image at 2K.
  • Seedance 2.5 for video, also in Dreamina. I used the Flash version at 1:1, 720p. It costs 17 points per second, and the 8 clips (62 seconds in total) came to about 1,054 points.
  • Any editor. CapCut is fine: hard cuts, white flashes and a song.

5 steps

  1. Story: the ending is the grills

    Pick one image the viewer will remember and build the reel backward from it. Mine is a smile full of gold grills with a tiny arrowhead on one tooth. Then give the story one thread you can see in every scene. Here it is a long red feather: in his hood, on the arrows, in the sheriff's hat, and at the end behind Grandma's ear.
    1. Hook: a mugshot. Robie holds the placard, a giant stands behind him with his head out of frame.
    2. The shot: an arrow takes the wig off the rich man and pins it to a palm tree.
    3. The heist: the vault opens, the giant rips the cash machine out of the wall.
    4. The chase: a church van, police cars, the sheriff loses his hat to an arrow.
    5. The give: money rains over the poor street.
    6. The ending: the arrest, the crowd throws roses, and Robie grins with gold grills.

    Check every still before you animate it: count the people, read the text on the placard, look at the hands. Text only works when it is short and the camera stays still on it.

  4. Video: multi-shot clips

    1. Mark every shot with its seconds and close it with "Hard cut". Start the next one with "Cut to:", or the camera drifts instead of cutting.
    2. One camera move per shot, in plain words: crane up, push-in, locked-off, handheld. Say it in its own sentence, apart from the action.
    3. Write the audio yourself. Name each sound next to what makes it, and say "no music, no dialogue, no subtitles". The song goes on in the edit.
    4. Keep shots over 1.5 seconds in the prompt. Shorter ones get rushed or skipped. Generate them a bit longer and trim in the edit.
    5. List what must not appear at the end: text, logos, extra people, face changes.

  5. The edit: cut on the beat, add sound

    I measured the edits of four reels in this niche. Almost everything that looks like an effect (push-ins, whips, a hand covering the lens, paparazzi flashes) is already inside the AI clip. The edit itself is small:
    • Hard cuts on the beat. No dissolves, no fades. Cut a shot as soon as its action has landed.
    • White flashes of 2 frames on the last frames of a shot, then a hard cut to the next one. Longer flashes look cheap.
    • A contrasty grade. Crush the blacks a little, keep the colour of the light in each scene.
    • No added grain and no fake camera shake. I tried both on my first cut, and it looked more like AI. The references have neither.
    • Sound. I made 22 sound effects with ElevenLabs: arrows, glass, the vault door, sirens, the camera flash. The music is a song from the Instagram library, started so the drop lands on the flash at 2 seconds.
    • The CTA: a small label at the bottom for the last second, away from the face.

Common mistakes

  • Too many shots for one clip. One of my clips holds five shots, so I generated it at 10 seconds and trimmed it in the edit.
  • The first frame is a little different. With several references the model only roughly keeps your start frame. Describe the framing again in the prompt.
  • A filter rejects one of your images. Dreamina turned down my vault image. I deleted it and described the vault in text instead.
  • A character looks like a real person. My first rich man looked like a known public figure, so I remade him from scratch. Check your sheets for this.
  • A white flash as the first frame. If your clip opens on a camera flash, trim it, or the reel starts on a blank frame.
