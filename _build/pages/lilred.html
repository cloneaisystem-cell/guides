This is CASE 001 of my series Once Upon a Crime: fairy-tale characters as real-world criminals. The famous line of the tale becomes the punchline. Here, "what big teeth you have" turns into her gold grills.

What you need

  • An image model that does real skin. I used GPT Image 2.5 inside Melius (about 70 credits per image, 80 with a reference).
  • Seedance 2.5 for video. Also inside Melius: 6 seconds at 480p Draft cost 752 credits per clip. The whole reel came to about 7,900 Melius credits.
  • Any editor. CapCut is fine: hard cuts, a zoom and a song.

5 steps

  1. Story: the famous line becomes the ending

    Pick the one line everybody knows from the tale and turn it into the last picture. Then build the middle as a trap that flips: the wolf thinks he is the hunter, the girl and her grandma are the real gang.
    1. Hook: two mugshots, the wolf in grandma's nightgown, then Red popping her gum.
    2. Her world: she walks her street, Grandma nods from the porch with a shotgun on her knees, the basket is full of cash.
    3. The wolf: stalks her, runs ahead, waits in Grandma's bed.
    4. "What big...": three macros, eyes, ears, teeth.
    5. The flip: Grandma steps out of the wardrobe and racks the shotgun. The wolf raises his paws.
    6. The ending: the getaway, then the mugshot: she grins with gold grills.

  2. Character sheets

    One sheet per character: front, three-quarter, back, close-up, profile and three expressions on a grey background. Every later image and clip uses these as references, so the faces stay the same. My template (swap the brackets): 
    Photorealistic character reference sheet of the same [WHO: woman about 23, olive skin, light freckles, curly auburn hair], five views on a neutral light-grey studio background:
1. Full body front, head to feet, neutral stance, holding [A PROP FOR SCALE: a wicker basket with a red-and-white gingham cloth].
2. Full body three-quarter view left.
3. Full body rear view.
4. Head-and-shoulders close-up, straight-on.
5. Head-and-shoulders 90-degree profile.
Bottom row: three small head crops with expressions: [calm neutral, crooked smirk, raised eyebrow].
Wardrobe: [short red velvet hooded cape, white ribbed tank top, loose light-wash jeans, plain white sneakers with no logos, large thin gold hoop earrings].
Real skin texture: visible pores, uneven skin tone, fine baby hairs, a few flyaway curls, no retouching, no glamorization, no beauty filter.
Clean soft studio light, key upper left, fill right. Identical identity, proportions and costume in every view. Photoreal, not illustrated. No text, no labels, no logos, no watermark, no extra people.

    For the wolf I wrote "an anthropomorphic storybook wolf made real, like a high-end practical film creature photographed on set, not CGI-looking, not a mascot costume": upright man's body, a real grey wolf head, clawed hands and a tail, in Grandma's pink nightgown, lace night cap and round glasses.

  3. Start frames: one per clip, each with a "device"

    Every clip starts from a still made from the sheets. Give each scene its own camera look so the reel feels filmed, not generated: police flash, doorbell camera, security camera, dashcam, telephoto through branches. The mugshot: 
    Image 1 = identity reference (face, freckles, curly auburn hair, red velvet hooded cape, gold hoop earrings); ignore its background and layout. Photorealistic police booking photograph, vertical 9:16. The same woman from Image 1 stands centred facing the camera in front of a pale grey-green wall with painted black height-chart lines, hood up, holding a black letter-board placard at chest height reading exactly "LIL RED" and below it "CASE 001". She is blowing a large pink bubble-gum bubble, eyes calm, one eyebrow slightly raised. Harsh frontal on-camera flash, hard shadow on the wall, flat dull colours, old compact digital camera, mild noise. Real skin texture: pores, freckles, flyaway curls, slight shine on the nose, no retouching, no beauty filter. No other text, no logos, no watermark, no other people.

    Check every still before you animate it: count the people, read the text on the placard, look at the hands.

  4. Video: multi-shot clips

    The viral reels in this niche cut a new shot every 1.6 seconds. Instead of one clip per shot, I asked Seedance 2.5 for 2 to 3 shots inside one 6-second clip, with the start frame and the sheets as references. Same light, same faces, three angles. The hook clip: 
    ASSET BINDING: Image 1 = the anthropomorphic wolf (upright man's body, real grey wolf head, furry clawed hands, pink floral nightgown, white lace night cap, round wire glasses) holding the placard "B.B. WOLF / CASE 001" in the police booking room. Image 2 = the young woman in the red velvet hood with gold hoop earrings holding the placard "LIL RED / CASE 001".
SUMMARY: Police booking room, two mugshots back to back: a guilty wolf in grandma's nightgown, then a calm young woman in a red hood. Deadpan comedy.
STYLE: photoreal vertical 9:16 police booking camera footage, harsh frontal flash, old compact digital camera look, mild noise, real-time speed, no slow motion.
SHOT 1 [0-3s] medium shot, eye level, static, straight-on, exact framing and look of Image 1: the wolf stands holding the placard; the camera flash fires and the frame flashes white; he blinks, his ears flatten, and with one clawed finger he nervously pushes his round glasses up his snout while glancing sideways, embarrassed. HARD CUT.
SHOT 2 [3-6s] medium close-up, eye level, static, same grey-green height-chart wall, exact look of Image 2: the young woman in the red velvet hood holds the LIL RED placard and chews gum; she blows a big pink bubble, the flash fires and the bubble pops on her lips; she smirks slowly with one eyebrow raised, totally unbothered.
CONSISTENCY: placard text stays exactly as in the images, same faces and wardrobe, the wolf keeps real fur and a real wolf head, five fingers per hand, no extra people.
AUDIO: camera flash pops with capacitor whine, a small nervous whimper from the wolf, gum chewing, bubble pop, quiet room tone; no music, no dialogue, no subtitles.
    1. Bind every image to one job: "Image 1 = exact first frame", "Image 2 = her identity". Two well-labelled references beat five vague ones.
    2. Give each shot its own camera line: size, angle, lens feel and one move. End it with HARD CUT.
    3. One action per shot, ending on something you can see: the bubble pops, the door swings shut.
    4. Ask for sound but no music: real sounds tied to the action, "no music, no dialogue, no subtitles". The song goes on in the edit.
    5. Draft at 480p first. You only use 1 to 2 seconds of each shot, so Draft quality holds up after a light upscale.

  5. The edit: cut on the beat

    Put the clips over the song and cut every shot on a beat, 21 cuts in 28 seconds. The drop lands on the first cut after the two mugshots, and Red's bubble pops right on it. Cut times in seconds:

    • Turn the colour down. AI clips come out too saturated. I measured the viral reels and mine: mine had about 1.5 times their saturation, so I cut it by a third and lifted the shadows a little.
    • Vary the shot lengths. Short, short, then one longer hold on the biggest moment. All shots at the same length feels like a metronome.
    • Snap zooms on the mugshots in your editor, right on the flash.
    • End on the face. The last shot looks into the lens and holds for 4 seconds.

Common mistakes

  • Four shots in one 6-second clip. Every shot gets 1.5 seconds and the model rushes the action. Two or three shots per clip is the limit.
  • Small details on a costume creature. Glasses on a snout or gum on a muzzle can vanish. Keep the gag big and easy to read.
  • Continuity slips between clips. In my porch shot Grandma wears a grey cardigan instead of her pink housecoat. Put the sheet of every character in the scene into the references, not only the start frame.
  • The first frame is a white flash. If your clip starts on a camera flash, trim it, or the reel opens on a blank frame.
